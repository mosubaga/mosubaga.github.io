A Local LLM as a Study Partner

What a local model can do with your textbooks, what you need to install, and where it falls short.

Answer: yes, and it can stay offline

A model running on Ollama can explain a concept, quiz you, and turn a chapter into flashcards. On its own it only knows its training data, so it does not know your textbook, your lecturer's notation, or which topics are on your exam. You close that gap by giving it the text of your PDFs, either a section at a time or through a search index over the whole book.

Unlike web search, this workflow needs no internet connection once the models are downloaded. The PDF, your questions and the answers all stay on your computer.

1. Why a local model

  • Privacy. Lecture notes, draft thesis chapters and unpublished papers are not uploaded to anyone.
  • No per-question cost. You can ask the same thing five ways, or generate hundreds of practice questions.
  • Works offline, for example in a library with poor Wi-Fi or on a flight.
  • The trade-off is quality. A model that fits on a laptop is weaker than a large hosted model. It makes more mistakes, especially in long calculations, so the workflow in this handbook always ties answers back to a page you can check.

Use the exact tags shown by ollama list on your machine. Larger chat models answer better but need more memory; download size is not the same as runtime memory use, and a larger context window needs more again. Browse alternatives in the Ollama model registry and test them on your own material.

RAG stands for retrieval-augmented generation. Both approaches start with the same step, getting clean text out of the PDF, which is chapter 02.

4. What it is good and bad at

Works wellNeeds careDo not rely on it
Explaining a passage in simpler wordsMulti-step derivations and proofsArithmetic and long calculations
Writing practice questions from a textDates, names and exact figuresCitations it produces from memory
Turning a section into flashcardsFormulas extracted from PDFsAnything not in the pages you gave it
Checking your own explanation of an ideaSummaries of very long chaptersBeing the only source before an exam

Important: A fluent answer is not a correct answer. Treat the model as a study partner who has skimmed the book: useful for practice and explanation, but the textbook is the authority. Every example here prints page numbers so you can check.

5. Studying with it, not instead of studying

The techniques in chapter 04 are built around retrieval practice: trying to recall and explain material yourself, then checking. A model that hands you a summary to reread helps much less than one that asks you questions. Use it to generate practice, not to replace reading.

Check your course rules as well. Using a model to study is different from submitting its output as your own work, and many institutions have explicit policies on both.
