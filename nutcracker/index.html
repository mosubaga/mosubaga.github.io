What is it?

A Fairy Tale Set to Music

A Gift That Comes Alive

On Christmas Eve, a young girl named Clara receives an odd wooden nutcracker doll from her mysterious godfather. That night, after the party guests go home, the doll — and every toy in the room — comes to life. What follows is a battle against an army of mice, a snowy journey, and a magical kingdom made entirely of sweets.

For All Ages

Part fairy tale, part fantasy, part family tradition: The Nutcracker has no spoken dialogue, so the orchestra and the dancers tell the whole story. Children follow Clara's adventure wide-eyed, while adults return year after year for the music, the craft, and the nostalgia of the holidays.

A Quick History

From St. Petersburg to the World

✦

1892 — The Premiere

Composer Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky and choreographer Marius Petipa (whose assistant, Lev Ivanov, completed much of the staging) debuted The Nutcracker at the Mariinsky Theatre in St. Petersburg, Russia, on December 18, 1892. It was loosely based on E. T. A. Hoffmann's story The Nutcracker and the Mouse King, as softened into a gentler retelling by Alexandre Dumas.

A Rocky Start

Believe it or not, the first audiences and critics were lukewarm. Tchaikovsky himself doubted the piece. Nobody in that theater could have guessed it would become one of the most performed ballets on Earth.

Becoming a Christmas Tradition

The ballet found new life in America in the 20th century — San Francisco Ballet staged the first full-length U.S. production in 1944, and George Balanchine's beloved 1954 New York City Ballet version cemented it as an annual holiday ritual for families across the country, and eventually the world.
