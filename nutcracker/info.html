Did You Know?

Behind the Scenes

  1. 01A brand-new instrument, kept secret. For the famous "Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy," Tchaikovsky used the celesta — a instrument so new he had it smuggled into Russia, hoping to use its shimmering sound before any rival composer could.
  2. 02It wasn't a hit at first. The 1892 premiere in St. Petersburg got mixed reviews. Tchaikovsky himself thought it was weaker than his other work — yet today it's one of the most performed ballets in the world.
  3. 03Two authors, one story. The ballet is based on E. T. A. Hoffmann's 1816 tale The Nutcracker and the Mouse King, but the version Petipa actually used was a softer, friendlier retelling written by Alexandre Dumas père in 1844.
  4. 04The choreographer never finished it. Marius Petipa fell ill during the creation process, so his assistant, Lev Ivanov, completed much of the actual choreography — including the famous "Waltz of the Snowflakes."
  5. 05America made it a tradition. It took George Balanchine's 1954 New York City Ballet production to turn The Nutcracker into the annual holiday ritual it is today across the United States.
  6. 06A costume built for a crowd. Mother Ginger's enormous hoop skirt isn't just for show — it conceals several child dancers (the Polichinelles), who burst out from underneath it.
  7. 07A ballet for beginners. The party-scene children and the roles of Clara and Fritz are often danced by young students, making this one of the few classical ballets built around a stage full of kids.
  8. 08No words needed. Like most classical ballets, there's no spoken dialogue at all — every twist of the story is told through Tchaikovsky's score and the dancers' movement.

Going to See It?

A Few Friendly Tips

Runtime

Most productions run about two hours with one intermission — plenty of room for little ones to stretch their legs.

What to Wear

Dress code varies by theater, but many families treat it as a festive occasion — think holiday best, not strict formal wear.

First-Timers Welcome

There's no dialogue to follow and no prior knowledge required — the story is told clearly enough for a five-year-old and a grandparent to enjoy side by side.
